# Trademark policy

"Orbital", "Call Theory", and the Orbital and Call Theory logos are
trademarks of Call Theory.

The AGPL covers copyright. It says nothing about names, and deliberately so —
a free software license does not give you the right to imply that your build
is our build. This page says what we will and will not object to, so that you
do not have to guess.

## You do not need permission to

- Run Orbital, modified or unmodified, and call it Orbital when describing
  what you run — "we run Orbital", "hosted on Orbital", "an Orbital
  deployment".
- Say your product integrates with, extends, or is compatible with Orbital,
  as long as the phrasing does not suggest we built or endorsed it.
- Use the name in documentation, blog posts, talks, comparisons, and reviews,
  favourable or not.
- Keep the name in an unmodified fork you are hosting for your own use, or
  distributing without changes.
- Use the logo to link to the upstream project.

## You do need permission to

- **Ship modified Orbital under the name "Orbital."** Fork it, change it,
  distribute it — that is your right, and we encourage it. Name it something
  else. If your version has our name on it, your bugs become our support
  burden and our reputation, and we have no way to fix them.
- Use "Orbital" or "Call Theory" in a product name, company name, domain
  name, or app store listing.
- Use our logos as your own product's branding.
- Say or imply that your service is "official", "certified", or endorsed by
  us when it is not.

## Rebranding a fork

Orbital is built to be rebranded — the Platform Branding and Portal Branding
admin pages exist for exactly this. Set your own name, logo, and colours
there, and change `ORBITAL_SOURCE_URL` to point at your source repository so
your users' AGPL section 13 offer resolves to your code rather than ours.

Nothing in this policy restricts what the AGPL grants you. If the two ever
appear to conflict, the AGPL wins on copyright and this policy is limited to
what trademark law actually reaches.
