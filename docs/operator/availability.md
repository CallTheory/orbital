# Availability

Your availability controls whether new work reaches you. It is the first
thing you set at the start of a shift and the last thing you change at the
end of one.

## Setting your status

The availability selector sits in the operator panel's top bar and is always
visible. Changing it takes effect immediately — there is no save step.

| Status | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Available** | Calls ring you and queued work is offered to you |
| A **reason** — Break, Lunch, Training, Meeting, and whatever else your platform has configured | You stop receiving new work |

The list of non-available reasons is set by your administrator, so it
matches how your call center actually talks about time off the phones.

## Reasons that block calls

Not every reason takes you off the phones. Your administrator can configure
a reason either way:

- **Blocking** — you receive no calls. Break and Lunch normally work this way.
- **Non-blocking** — you are marked as busy for reporting, but calls still
  reach you. Useful for states like "Paperwork" where you are at your desk
  and can still pick up.

If you are unsure which a reason is, watch whether calls keep arriving after
you select it.

## What availability does not do

**It does not release work you have already claimed.** An email thread or
text conversation you claimed stays yours while you are on break. If you are
leaving for the day with claimed work outstanding, release it explicitly so
somebody else can pick it up — see [Email Inbox](email-inbox.md) and
[Message Inbox](message-inbox.md).

**It does not end an active call.** Going unavailable stops the *next* call
from arriving; it does not interrupt the one you are on.

## Logging out

When you log out you may be asked for a reason — end of shift, system issue,
and so on. This is how the platform distinguishes a normal end of shift from
somebody dropping off unexpectedly, which matters when your supervisor is
looking at coverage later.
